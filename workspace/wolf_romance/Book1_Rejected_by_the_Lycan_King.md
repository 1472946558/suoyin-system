## Prologue — The Night Everything Burned

I was seven years old the night my world ended.

The smell came first—smoke and copper, the kind of scent that burrows into your lungs and never leaves. I was in bed, my mother's lullaby still warm in my ears, when the first scream tore through the Silverfang settlement.

My mother burst through the door, her silver hair wild, her violet eyes blazing with a terror I'd never seen in her before. Not when the hunters came. Not when the river flooded our homes. This was different. This was the end.

"Elara." She scooped me up, her hands shaking. "Listen to me. You must be silent. Not a sound. Not a single breath."

Through the window, I saw them—massive wolves in black, pouring through our gates like shadows given form. They moved with military precision, cutting down everything in their path. Our warriors fell. Our homes burned. The Alpha's howl cut off mid-note.

My mother carried me through the chaos, her body shielding mine from the heat and the horror. She shoved me into a hollow space beneath the old oak—the one where I used to hide when I played with the other pups. The space smelled like earth and moss and safety.

"Mama—"

"Shh." She pressed something into my palm. A silver pendant, shaped like a crescent moon, cold against my skin. "Never take this off. Never let them know what you are."

"What am I?"

Her eyes glistened. She kissed my forehead, and her lips were warm, and for a moment, everything was okay.

"You are everything, my love. And nothing they deserve to know."

She sealed the hollow with branches and leaves. I pressed my face to the gap and watched her walk back toward the flames. She didn't look back. I think she knew that if she did, she'd never be able to leave me behind.

The screams lasted hours. I didn't make a single sound.

When the black wolves found me at dawn, I was curled in the dirt, clutching the pendant so hard it left a crescent moon imprint in my palm.

"An Omega pup," one of them said, his voice rough with exhaustion. "No threat."

They sent me to the Redfang Pack—largest pack in the territory, known for taking in strays. For orphans. For wolves nobody wanted.

I spent the next eleven years learning exactly what it meant to be nothing.

## Chapter 1 — The Omega's Place

The Blood Moon was three days away, and the entire Redfang Pack had lost its collective mind.

Every surface was being scrubbed. Every garment pressed. The cook, a heavyset woman named Hilda, had been baking for forty-eight hours straight, and the Great Hall smelled like cinnamon and desperation. Even the pups were cleaning—wielding brooms half their height, sweeping dirt from one corner to another with more enthusiasm than skill.

And me? I was elbow-deep in dishwater.

"Elara." Hilda's voice cut through the kitchen chaos. "More plates. The Lycan King's envoy will be here by nightfall, and if I don't have enough platters for the welcoming feast, I will personally feed you to the Elders."

"Yes, Hilda." I pulled my hands from the water, wrung them dry on my apron, and went to fetch the stack of ceremonial plates from the back pantry.

The pantry was cold and dark, stacked floor to ceiling with preserved meats and jars of pickled vegetables. I found the plates on the top shelf—of course—and stretched onto my toes to reach them. My fingers barely grazed the edge.

"At least the King's coming," I muttered to myself, dragging a crate over to stand on. "Nobody cares if the Omega can reach the good plates."

That was the thing about being an Omega in a pack like Redfang. You weren't quite a servant, but you weren't quite a member either. You existed in the spaces between—cleaning up after everyone else, eating last, speaking least. Eleven years I'd been here, and I still got looks that said, *Oh right. You live here.*

I grabbed the plates and headed back to the kitchen, navigating through the chaos of bodies and steam and shouted orders. A young she-wolf named Petra collided with me, sending two plates crashing to the floor.

"Watch it, Omega!" Petra snapped, even though she was the one who'd been walking backward. She looked at the shattered porcelain, then at me, and her expression shifted from anger to calculation. "You'll clean that up. And you'll tell Hilda it was your fault."

I looked at her. She was two years younger than me, pretty in a sharp-featured way, and already promised to the pack's Head Warrior. She'd never liked me—too many years of treating me like a convenient target.

"It was your fault," I said. Not because I expected it to matter. But because I was tired of swallowing lies that weren't mine.

Petra's eyes flashed. "You forget your place."

"My place is carrying plates for people who can't be bothered to look where they're walking." I crouched down and started picking up the shards. "Same as always."

She made a sound of disgust and walked away. I gathered the broken pieces carefully, adding a small cut to my finger in the process, and dumped them in the refuse bin.

The Blood Moon ceremony was the biggest event in a decade. Every pack in the territory was sending representatives. And the Lycan King himself—Kael Thorne, ruler of all wolf-kind—was coming to Redfang to find his mate.

Or rather, to find a mate. The Moon Goddess would choose. That was how it worked. Under the Blood Moon, fated mates were revealed. Bonds awakened. Lives intertwined by forces older than memory.

For most wolves, it was the most important night of their lives.

For an Omega like me, it was just another night of watching from the edges.

I wasn't supposed to have a mate. Omegas rarely did. We were the lowest of the low—our wolves small, our instincts muted, our worth measured in how well we served others. The Moon Goddess didn't waste fate on wolves like me.

And honestly? That was fine. I'd stopped believing in fate the night my mother walked into the flames.

I returned the surviving plates to Hilda, bandaged my finger with a strip of cloth, and went back to the dishes. Through the kitchen window, I could see the sun setting over the tree line, painting the sky in shades of amber and rose.

Three days. Three days until the Blood Moon rose and everyone else's lives changed.

Mine would stay exactly the same.

## Chapter 2 — The King Arrives

He arrived at dusk, like something out of the old stories.

I was hauling water from the well when the sound rolled through the settlement—boots on stone, horses' hooves, the low hum of power that preceded any high-ranking wolf. The air itself changed, growing heavier, charged with the kind of energy that made every wolf in the pack sit up and pay attention.

I set down the buckets and moved to the edge of the crowd, staying in the back where Omegas belonged.

The Lycan King's procession entered through the main gate. Warriors first—tall, dark-clad, moving with the fluid grace of predators. Then came the advisors and officials, their faces stern, their postures rigid. And finally, at the center, flanked by his Beta and two Guards—

Kael Thorne.

I'd heard descriptions, of course. Everyone had. The Lycan King was the most powerful wolf in generations—ruthless in battle, brilliant in politics, cold as the winter moon in his dealings. He'd taken the throne at eighteen after his father's assassination and had held it for ten years through sheer force of will. And blood. There was always blood.

But descriptions hadn't prepared me for the reality of him.

He was tall—over six feet—with dark hair swept back from a face carved from granite and shadow. His jaw was sharp enough to cut. His eyes were gold—not warm gold, not soft gold, but the hard, bright gold of a predator watching its territory. He wore black from throat to boot, and even from a distance, I could feel the power radiating off him like heat from a furnace.

He moved through the crowd the way a blade moves through silk—effortlessly, leaving silence in his wake. The Redfang Alpha, Marcus, stepped forward to greet him with a bow that was just slightly too low to be dignified.

"Lycan King Thorne. Redfang Pack is honored beyond words."

Kael inclined his head a fraction of an inch. "Alpha Marcus. Your hospitality is noted."

His voice was low and smooth, the kind of voice that made you lean in to hear better—and then regret it when you realized what you were hearing.

I watched from behind a pillar as the formal greetings droned on. Elders fawned. Warriors postured. Petra and the other she-wolves preened and whispered, positioning themselves along the King's path like flowers arranged for display.

The King's Beta—a man with dark skin and watchful eyes—leaned close to Kael and murmured something. Kael's gaze swept the crowd, a brief flick of gold that missed nothing.

For one heartbeat—just one—his eyes passed over the pillar where I stood.

Our gazes didn't meet. He didn't see me. I was a shadow in a room full of light, invisible by design and by habit.

But something tightened in my chest. A pull. Brief, faint, like a thread I'd grabbed without meaning to.

I let go of the thread immediately.

Whatever that was, it wasn't for me. Nothing like that was ever for me.

I picked up my buckets and went back to the kitchen.

## Chapter 3 — Moonstruck

The Blood Moon rose like a wound in the sky.

I'd never seen one this close. The elders said it only happened once every fifty years—a moon so red it looked like the sky was bleeding, and under its light, the mate bond awakened with devastating force. No wolf could resist it. No wolf could hide.

The entire pack—plus two hundred visiting wolves—gathered in the ceremonial circle. Torches lined the perimeter, their flames wavering in the cool night air. At the center stood the Moon Stone, an ancient slab of silver-veined rock that predated the pack itself.

The mated pairs went first, reaffirming their bonds under the Blood Moon's light. Then the unmated—warriors, she-wolves, everyone of age arranged in concentric rings around the stone, waiting for the Moon Goddess to reveal their fate.

I stood in the outermost ring. Last row. Last place. The Omega position.

I didn't expect anything. I wanted it to be over so I could go back to the kitchen and finish the cleanup.

The Blood Moon climbed higher. Its light hit the Moon Stone, and the silver veins began to glow. A low hum filled the air—the sound of magic, ancient and inevitable.

Around me, wolves began to stir. Gasps. Whispers. A young warrior across the circle locked eyes with a she-wolf from a visiting pack, and they both went rigid, the bond slamming into them like a physical force. They moved toward each other, drawn by something invisible and undeniable.

*Mates.*

It happened again and again. Wolves finding each other, their faces transforming with wonder, with shock, with joy. The air was thick with it—the electric charge of fated bonds awakening, of destinies being written.

I watched, hollow and envious, trying to tell myself I felt nothing.

And then—

My chest seized.

Not pain, exactly. More like a hand reaching inside me and gripping something I didn't know existed. A cord I'd never felt before pulled taut, vibrating with a frequency that made my bones hum.

*No.*

My wolf—small, quiet, barely a whisper in my mind—suddenly *roared*. A sound I'd never heard from her, raw and primal and desperate.

**MATE.**

The pull yanked my head up. My eyes flew across the circle, past the warriors and the she-wolves and the Elders and the torches, past everything, until they landed—

On gold.

Kael Thorne was already looking at me.

The world stopped. The noise, the crowd, the torches, the moon—all of it fell away, replaced by a silence so complete I could hear my own heartbeat. Or maybe it was his. I couldn't tell anymore.

The bond ignited between us like a wildfire—hot, fast, consuming. Every cell in my body screamed **MINE**. My wolf was howling, pacing, frantic with a need I didn't understand. I could feel *him*—his strength, his power, the barely leashed predator beneath his skin.

And he could feel me.

I saw it in his face. The flicker of shock. The widening of those golden eyes. The way his jaw tightened and his hands curled into fists at his sides.

*The Lycan King. My mate.*

The most powerful wolf in the world.

And me. The Omega. The nothing.

The silence spread outward from us like a shockwave. One by one, the wolves around us stopped. Followed the line of sight. Saw where the King was looking.

Saw *me*.

A thousand eyes. A thousand thoughts I could feel pressing against my skin like thorns.

*The Omega? His mate? Impossible.*

*She's nobody.*

*This has to be a mistake.*

The weight of their judgment settled over me like a funeral shroud. I couldn't breathe. I couldn't move. All I could do was stand there, trembling, waiting for the King to say something—anything—that would make this real.

Kael took a step forward.

My heart flew into my throat.

He was coming toward me. The crowd parted like water, wolves scrambling to get out of his path. Each footstep was a hammer blow. His eyes never left mine—gold burning into violet, predator meeting prey, fate meeting destiny.

He stopped three feet away from me.

Close enough that I could smell him—pine and smoke and something darker, something that made my wolf whimper with want. Close enough that I could see the pulse jumping in his throat.

Close enough to reject me.

## Chapter 4 — The Rejection

The silence lasted exactly three heartbeats.

Then Kael Thorne, Lycan King, ruler of all wolf-kind, looked at me—really looked at me—saw the Omega, the nothing, the silver-haired orphan who washed dishes and hauled water and existed in the margins of everyone else's life—and made his choice.

His expression closed. Like a door slamming shut. Like a blade sliding into its sheath.

"I, Kael Thorne, Lycan King, Alpha of Alphas—" His voice cut through the silence, cold and clear and absolute. "—reject this bond."

The words hit me like a physical blow.

"She is not my mate."

He turned away. Just like that. One sentence and he was done, facing the crowd instead of me, his back a wall of black fabric and colder intent.

The mate bond—the one that had blazed to life moments ago—*shredded*.

I can't describe the pain to anyone who hasn't felt it. Imagine someone reaching into your chest, wrapping their fingers around your soul, and pulling. Not gently. Not quickly. A slow, deliberate tear, like ripping cloth that doesn't want to come apart. Every nerve ending screamed. My vision blurred. My knees buckled.

I hit the ground.

The sound I made wasn't a scream. It was something worse—a raw, broken gasp, the sound of a wolf whose mate has just told her she's worthless.

Laughter. Someone was laughing. Not many—two or three, high and cruel. Petra's voice rose above the rest: "The Omega actually thought she could be Queen!"

Others weren't laughing. Some looked away, embarrassed. Some looked at me with pity, which was somehow worse. The Elders exchanged glances loaded with implications I couldn't parse through the pain.

The bond was gone. Ripped out by the roots. But something lingered—a ghost of the connection, a phantom limb that still reached for a man who'd just declared it didn't exist.

Kael hadn't moved. Hadn't looked back. He was speaking to Alpha Marcus now, his voice low and steady, as if nothing significant had happened. As if he hadn't just destroyed someone in front of five hundred witnesses.

I forced myself to my knees. Then to my feet. My legs were shaking. My vision was swimming. The pain was a living thing inside me, clawing to get out.

But I stood.

Darius—the King's Beta—appeared at my side. His dark eyes were unreadable, but his hand was steady as he offered it to me.

"Let me help you—"

"No." My voice came out rough, broken. I pushed his hand away. "I don't need help."

I straightened my spine. Lifted my chin. Turned my head until I was looking at Kael's back one last time.

The words came from somewhere deeper than my mind. Somewhere older. Somewhere that sounded like my mother's voice and my wolf's howl and every whispered insult I'd ever swallowed.

"You'll regret this."

It wasn't loud. It wasn't dramatic. But it was true—every syllable of it, forged in the fire of rejection and tempered by eleven years of being treated like nothing.

Kael's shoulders stiffened. Just barely. Just enough to tell me he'd heard.

He didn't turn around.

I walked away from the ceremony on my own two feet, past the torches and the whispering crowd, past the Moon Stone and its mocking silver glow, past every wolf who'd watched me be discarded like garbage.

I made it to the tree line before I collapsed.

The forest caught me. The dark, quiet, indifferent forest. I pressed my forehead to the cold ground and let the sobs come—deep, wrenching, tearing sobs that tasted like copper and ash and five hundred years of Silverfang blood I didn't even know I carried.

The mate bond was broken.

But the child growing inside me—the one I didn't know about yet, the one conceived in shadow and silence three nights ago—was just beginning.

## Chapter 5 — Shattered

Three days after the rejection, I still couldn't sleep.

Not because of the pain—though that was bad enough. The mate bond's severing left a hollow space in my chest that ached with every breath, like a wound that wouldn't close. The pack's healer, a graying woman named Thessaly, had given me a tonic for the physical symptoms. It helped with the shaking. It did nothing for the emptiness.

The real reason I couldn't sleep was the memories.

They came unbidden, every time I closed my eyes—fragments of the night before the Blood Moon ceremony, pieces I'd been trying not to think about.

The forest. The dark. The rogue wolf that had come out of nowhere, jaws snapping, eyes wild with the madness that consumed wolves who stayed too long without a pack. I'd been gathering herbs after dark—stupid, I knew, but Hilda had needed moonpetal for the feast and I'd volunteered because that's what Omegas did.

I'd frozen. My wolf was too small, too weak, to fight off a rogue. I was going to die in the dark, torn apart by a mad wolf, and no one would even notice I was gone until the dishes started piling up.

And then—

A blur of motion. A growl that shook the ground. A massive black wolf materializing from the shadows, slamming into the rogue with the force of a freight train. The rogue was dead before it hit the ground, its neck snapped with a sound that echoed through the trees.

The black wolf shifted.

Kael Thorne stood before me, naked, his chest heaving, his golden eyes glowing in the darkness. Moonlight turned his skin to silver and his hair to ink. He was beautiful and terrible and every inch a king.

For a long moment, neither of us spoke. The forest held its breath.

"You shouldn't be out here alone," he said finally. His voice was rougher than it had been in the ceremony—rawer, closer to his wolf.

"I was gathering herbs." I couldn't look away from him. My heart was pounding so hard I was sure he could hear it. "For the feast."

He looked at me—really looked, the same way he would three nights later under the Blood Moon. His gaze traced my face, my silver hair, my violet eyes. Something flickered in his expression. Recognition, maybe. Or its shadow.

"What's your name?"

"Elara."

"Elara." He repeated it like he was tasting the word. "You're the Omega."

Not a question. A statement. A classification.

"Yes."

He took a step closer. I should have moved away. Everything I knew about wolves and power and my place in the world told me to lower my eyes and retreat.

I didn't.

He was close enough to touch. Close enough that I could feel the heat radiating off his skin, could smell pine and smoke and something underneath that made my wolf stir in a way she'd never stirred before.

His hand came up. Slowly. His fingers brushed a strand of silver hair away from my face, and the touch was so gentle, so unexpected, that my breath caught.

Something electric passed between us. A spark. A prelude.

And then his mouth was on mine.

I didn't know who moved first. Maybe both of us. Maybe neither—maybe the bond that hadn't even awakened yet was already pulling us together, already writing a story that neither of us had agreed to.

His kiss was hard and desperate and tasted like starvation. My hands fisted in his hair. His arms wrapped around me, lifting me off my feet, pressing me against a tree trunk. The bark bit into my back and I didn't care. His body was fire and stone and I wanted—

*I wanted.*

I pulled away first. Gasping. My lips swollen, my heart racing, my wolf howling inside my head.

"I can't," I whispered. "You're the King. I'm—"

"I know what you are." His voice was rough. His eyes were gold fire. His hands still held my waist, and he wasn't letting go.

I made myself push him away. I ran back through the forest without looking back, my pulse roaring in my ears, his taste still on my lips.

That was three nights before the Blood Moon. Three nights before he stood in front of five hundred wolves and rejected me like I was nothing.

Three nights before I learned that you could share the most intense moment of your life with someone, and they could still throw you away.

Now I lay in my narrow cot in the Omega quarters, staring at the ceiling, one hand resting on my stomach.

Something was wrong. My body felt different—not just the hollow ache of the rejected bond, but something else. A heaviness. A warmth. A strange, persistent nausea that had started the morning after the ceremony and hadn't gone away.

I pressed my palm flat against my abdomen and closed my eyes.

*It's stress,* I told myself. *The rejection. The bond breaking. Your body is in shock.*

But deep in my bones, in the place where my small, quiet wolf lived, I felt the truth I wasn't ready to face.

A new heartbeat. Tiny. Fragile. Already growing.

## Chapter 6 — The Night That Changed Everything

I kept the secret for a week.

Seven days of nausea I couldn't explain away. Seven days of waking at dawn with a sick heaviness in my gut that had nothing to do with the rejected mate bond. Seven days of pressing my hand to my stomach and willing myself to be wrong.

On the eighth day, I stole a testing kit from the healer's supply room.

The result was immediate and unequivocal. Two lines. Crimson against white. The same color as the Blood Moon that had ruined my life.

Pregnant.

I was pregnant with the Lycan King's child.

I sat on the floor of the supply room, my back against the shelves, the test clutched in both hands, and I tried to think. Really think. Not panic—think.

The facts: I'd been with Kael once. In the forest. A collision of desperation and instinct that neither of us had planned. One kiss that had spiraled into something more—something reckless and consuming and utterly, catastrophically stupid.

The consequences: A child. His child. The heir to the Lycan throne growing inside the body of an Omega nobody wanted.

If the Redfang Pack found out, I'd become a pawn. Alpha Marcus would hand me over to the King in chains, using the child as leverage for political favor. If the King's Court found out, I'd become a target. A threat to every power structure that depended on the current succession.

And if Kael found out—

I didn't know what Kael would do. I didn't know him at all. The man who'd kissed me in the forest with desperate hunger was the same man who'd rejected me in front of five hundred witnesses without a flicker of hesitation. Which one was real? Probably neither. Probably both.

What I knew for certain was this: I could not stay.

Redfang was not safe. Nowhere in wolf territory was safe for an Omega carrying the Lycan King's secret heir. I needed to disappear—into the human world, where wolf politics meant nothing and a silver-haired woman with no pack could blend into the background.

I had money. Not much—a few coins from odd jobs, enough for a bus ticket. I had my mother's pendant, still hanging around my neck, its silver crescent warm against my skin. And I had the name of a town three hundred miles south where wolves never went: Ashwood.

I had to leave tonight.

---

The escape was easier than I expected. Omegas were invisible by nature—nobody watched us, nobody counted us, nobody noticed when one went missing. I packed a single bag: two changes of clothes, the pendant, the remaining coins, and a handful of dried herbs from the garden that I could sell in a human market.

I didn't say goodbye. There was no one to say goodbye to.

The forest swallowed me the way it had the night Kael saved me from the rogue. But this time, no one was coming to find me. No black wolf would materialize from the shadows. No golden eyes would track my path.

I was alone. Just me, the child growing inside me, and the ghost of a bond that refused to fully die.

I walked south through the night, following the river, staying off the main roads. My wolf—small, tired, grieving the mate bond's loss—kept watch through my senses, alerting me to every sound, every scent.

By dawn, I'd reached the edge of wolf territory. Beyond this point, the land belonged to humans. No packs. No politics. No Kings who kissed you in the dark and discarded you in the light.

I crossed the boundary line with my heart in my throat and my fists clenched at my sides.

*Goodbye,* I thought, not to Kael—never to Kael—but to the world that had never wanted me.

*Goodbye and good riddance.*

## Chapter 7 — The Discovery

Five years.

That's how long I stayed hidden. Five years of building a life in Ashwood, a small town nestled between rolling hills and farmland where the biggest excitement was the annual pumpkin festival and whether Mrs. Henderson's cat would get stuck in the church bell tower again (it did, every year, without fail).

I opened a flower shop. "Elara's Blooms"—a small storefront on Maple Street, wedged between a bakery and a used bookstore. The rent was cheap, the owner was kind, and flowers didn't care that I was a wolf without a pack.

Humans were easy, I discovered. They didn't smell the wolf beneath my skin. They didn't sense the power I kept leashed. They saw a quiet young woman with unusual silver hair and violet eyes and decided I was "artsy" and "a little mysterious" and left it at that.

I made friends. Maren, my next-door neighbor, a loud, warm human woman who taught kindergarten and believed firmly that every problem could be solved with either wine or baked goods (sometimes both). She didn't know what I was. She just knew I was lonely and she'd decided, with the immovable certainty of a kindergarten teacher, that I was going to be her friend whether I liked it or not.

I liked it. I liked her. I liked the life I'd built from nothing—the shop, the apartment above it, the routine of watering and pruning and arranging. It was small and safe and mine.

And then there was Nox.

My son.

He was born on a stormy night in March, in the back room of the flower shop because I couldn't risk a hospital—wolf births weren't human births, and I didn't know what a Lycan King's offspring might do. As it turned out, he did what all babies do: he screamed, he cried, and he changed everything.

Nox had my silver hair—fine and bright, falling across his forehead in a way that made old women in the grocery store stop and coo. But his eyes. His eyes were pure, molten gold.

His father's gold.

Every time I looked into those eyes, I saw Kael. The King who'd kissed me in darkness. The King who'd rejected me in light. The King who didn't know he had a son.

Nox was four now, and he was... different. Not in a way humans would notice—he was bright and funny and loved dinosaurs with a passion that bordered on religious. But I noticed. The way he could hear a car coming from three blocks away. The way he once lifted a bag of potting soil that weighed more than he did. The way he sometimes growled in his sleep—a low, rumbling sound that no four-year-old should be able to make.

He was a Lycan. His father's son. And no matter how deep I buried us in this human town, his blood would eventually call him home.

I just hoped I had more time.

"Mama!" Nox burst through the shop door, Maren's five-year-old daughter Lily trailing behind him. "Lily says dragons aren't real but I think they ARE because—"

"Because you found a really big lizard in the garden," I finished for him, lifting him onto the counter. "Which was an iguana, not a dragon."

"It could have been a baby dragon," Nox said, with the fierce conviction of a four-year-old who had never once doubted his own correctness.

"He's got a point," Maren said, appearing in the doorway with two cups of coffee. She handed one to me and gave Lily the Look that meant *stop encouraging the dragon theory*. "What? I'm keeping my options open."

I smiled—really smiled, the kind that reached my eyes—and for a moment, the world was simple. A flower shop. A best friend. A son with gold eyes and silver hair and a growl in his chest that spoke of something ancient and powerful.

For a moment, I almost forgot that I was running.

## Chapter 8 — The King's Shadow

**KAEL**

The dream came again.

Silver hair spread across dark earth. Violet eyes wide with shock. A gasp—broken, raw—that echoed through the forest like a dying song.

*I reject this bond.*

His own voice. His own words. A verdict delivered five years ago that still echoed through his skull every time he closed his eyes.

Kael Thorne opened his eyes.

His chambers were dark. The fire had burned to embers. Through the window, the moon hung fat and silver over the Lycan Court—the sprawling stone fortress that had housed wolf kings for three hundred years.

The mate bond was supposed to be severed. Broken. Gone. He'd done everything right—the formal words, the public declaration, the complete and deliberate withdrawal of his energy from the connection. The healers had confirmed it. The elders had documented it. By every measure that mattered, Kael Thorne had no mate.

So why could he still feel her?

Not the full bond—that was gone, or as close to gone as a rejected bond could get. But a whisper. A ghost. Like hearing music from a room you'd locked and couldn't re-enter. He'd been feeling it for five years, and it was getting worse, not better.

He rose from the bed and crossed to the window. The courtyard below was empty save for the night patrol—two warriors making their rounds in the moonlight. Beyond the walls, the forest stretched endlessly, dark and deep and full of wolves who answered to him.

He ruled all of it. Every pack, every territory, every wolf from the northern mountains to the southern coast. Ten years on the throne, and his power was absolute.

And yet, at three in the morning, he was standing at a window thinking about an Omega with silver hair.

*Kael Thorne, Lycan King, reject this bond.*

The words tasted like ash.

A knock on his door. "My King." Darius's voice, low and respectful. "Forgive the hour. We have a situation."

Kael opened the door. His Beta stood in the corridor, fully dressed, his dark face grave.

"Report."

"Our scouts picked up an anomaly in the southern human territories. A town called Ashwood." Darius hesitated—a rarity for him. "They detected a juvenile Lycan signature. Unregistered. Strong for a child."

Kael's jaw tightened. A Lycan child in human territory meant one of two things: a rogue wolf had bred with a human, or a she-wolf was hiding. Either way, it required investigation.

"Send a retrieval team."

"My King—" Darius paused again. "The signature is... distinctive. The scouts described it as 'royal-grade.' And the child appears to be approximately four years old."

Four years old. Royal-grade signature. Southern human territories.

Something cold slid down Kael's spine.

"Four years," he said slowly. "Which would place conception approximately five years ago."

"Yes, my King."

Five years ago. The Blood Moon ceremony at Redfang. The night he'd—

The night in the forest, before the ceremony. The Omega. The kiss. The heat.

*Impossible,* his mind insisted. *You rejected the bond. You severed the connection.*

But his wolf—his wolf had gone absolutely still inside him, the way it did before a kill. Quiet. Focused. Absolutely certain.

*MINE,* it snarled. Not a whisper. Not a ghost. A roar that shook his bones.

"I'll go myself," Kael said, and his voice was not the voice of a king making a strategic decision. It was the voice of a wolf who'd just scented prey.

## Chapter 9 — Five Years Later

**ELARA**

It was a Tuesday when everything fell apart again.

I remember because I'd just received a delivery ofpeonies—soft pink and cream, the kind that made the whole shop smell like spring—and Nox was in the back room drawing pictures of his "friends," which were actually very detailed and somewhat alarming depictions of wolves that he insisted visited him in his dreams.

My hand froze on the peony stems.

"What do you mean, baby?"

"The big wolf. The gold one. He's coming here."

*The gold one.*

Gold eyes. Gold like—

No.

"Because the big wolf is angry. And when the big wolf is angry, everyone runs. But I'm not running, Mama. I'm going to protect you."

My heart cracked right down the center.

I scooped him up, held him tight, and breathed in his scent—milk and crayons and something wild underneath, something that would only grow stronger as he got older.

"Nobody needs to protect anyone," I said, though every instinct I had was screaming that I was lying. "We're safe here."

I was wrong.

The black SUVs arrived at sundown.

## Chapter 10 — The Scent That Changed Everything

**KAEL**

Ashwood was exactly the kind of town Kael would never have visited voluntarily. Population 4,200. One main street. A bakery, a bookstore, and a flower shop called "Elara's Blooms."

*Elara.*

Her name was burned into his memory—the only piece of her he'd allowed himself to keep. Not the silver hair. Not the violet eyes. Just the name. Just the syllables. Just the way it had sounded when he'd spoken it aloud in the forest, tasting it like something rare and precious.

Five years of pretending it didn't matter. Five years of burying the ghost of the bond beneath politics and wars and the cold machinery of ruling. Five years of waking at 3 AM with the echo of her broken gasp ringing in his ears.

*I reject this bond.*

He'd had no choice. That's what he'd told himself. A Lycan King couldn't be mated to an Omega—it would destabilize the entire power structure. The elders would revolt. The council would fracture. Everything he'd spent a decade building would crumble.

So he'd done the logical thing. The strategic thing. The thing that a King was supposed to do.

He'd destroyed her.

And now he was sitting in the back of an SUV, driving through a human town to find the child he'd never known existed, and his hands were shaking.

Darius, in the passenger seat, glanced back at him. "My King—"

"Don't." Kael's voice was a blade. "Whatever you're about to say, don't."

Darius, who had been his Beta for eight years and had earned the right to say things others couldn't, said it anyway.

"You made the best decision you could with the information you had."

"The best decision." Kael's laugh was hollow. "I rejected my mate in front of five hundred witnesses and drove her into hiding. She raised my child alone for five years in a human town because she was afraid of what I would do." He met Darius's eyes. "What part of that was the best decision?"

Darius had no answer. There was no answer.

The SUV turned onto Maple Street.

Kael saw the flower shop first—small, warm, lit from within by golden light. Through the window, he could see buckets of flowers, a wooden counter, and the shadow of movement in the back room.

Then he saw her.

Elara stepped out the shop's back door, a bag over her shoulder, a child on her hip. She moved fast—faster than a human, faster than she should have been able to move with a four-year-old in her arms. She was heading for the alley behind the shop, and she was—

Running.

From him.

The sight of her hit him like a silver bullet. Silver hair longer than he remembered, flowing past her shoulders. A face sharper, leaner, harder than the girl he'd kissed in the forest. Violet eyes that had once looked at him with wonder and now blazed with something far more dangerous.

Fear. Determination. Fury.

She saw the SUV. Saw him through the tinted window. And her face—the brief flash of naked terror that crossed her features before she masked it with steel—was the worst thing he'd ever seen in his life.

He'd put that terror there. Him. His rejection. His throne. His pathetic, cowardly need to control everything around him.

The child in her arms turned his head.

Gold eyes met gold.

Kael's wolf went absolutely insane.

*MINE. MINE. MINE.*

Not just the woman. The *child*. His son. His blood. A miniature version of himself, right down to the jawline, held protectively against a woman who had every right to hate him for the rest of her life.

"Stop the car," Kael said.

"My King, we should approach this carefully—"

"I said stop the goddamn car."

The SUV braked. Kael was out before it fully stopped, his long stride eating the distance between the vehicle and the alley where Elara had frozen, her body curved protectively around their son.

"Don't come any closer," she said.

Her voice was steady. Her arms around the child were steel. And her eyes—those violet eyes that had haunted him for five years—were the eyes of a cornered wolf who would fight to the death for her pup.

He stopped.

Five feet between them. The child—*his son*—buried his face in Elara's neck, one small hand clutching her shirt. But even hiding, Kael could see it. The silver hair. The little body already humming with Lycan power.

"Elara." Her name came out rough, cracked, nothing like a King's command. "I didn't know."

"You didn't want to know." Her voice was ice. "You made that very clear, Kael. Under the Blood Moon. In front of everyone."

"I know."

"You *rejected* me."

"I know."

"You told five hundred people I wasn't your mate."

"I—" The words stuck in his throat. There was no defense. No justification. No version of this conversation where he wasn't the villain. "I know."

The child stirred. Lifted his head. Looked directly at Kael with golden eyes that saw everything.

"Are you the big wolf?" the boy asked.

Kael's heart stopped.

"Yes," he heard himself say. "Yes, I am."

The child—*his son*—studied him for a long moment, then turned to Elara and whispered, loud enough for Kael to hear:

"He's sad, Mama. The big wolf is very sad."

Elara closed her eyes. Pulled her son tighter.

And Kael Thorne, Lycan King, Alpha of Alphas, ruler of all wolf-kind, stood in a dark alley in a human town and felt, for the first time in ten years, the full weight of what he'd done.

He'd rejected his mate. He'd driven her away. And he'd missed the first five years of his son's life.

Some things couldn't be fixed with power. This was one of them.

But he was going to try anyway.

## Chapter 11 — The Messenger

**DARIUS**

Darius had served Kael Thorne for eight years. In that time, he'd seen his King execute traitors, outmaneuver rival packs, and hold the throne against challengers who thought a young King would be easy prey. He'd watched Kael make hard decisions without flinching, deliver judgments without wavering, and bury every emotion that wasn't useful.

But he had never—*never*—seen Kael Thorne's hands shake.

They were shaking now.

Darius stood at a respectful distance in the alley behind "Elara's Blooms," watching his King face the woman he'd rejected five years ago and the child who looked exactly like him. The boy—Nox, the mother had called him—was the spitting image of Kael at that age, if the portraits in the Lycan Court's gallery were to be believed. Same dark brows. Same jaw. Same gold eyes that seemed to see straight through a man's defenses.

The woman, Elara, was something else entirely. Darius had expected a cowering Omega, grateful for any attention from the King. That's what Omegas did—they submitted, they bowed, they made themselves small.

Elara did none of those things.

She stood in that alley like a cornered wolf—back to the wall, child in her arms, eyes blazing with a defiance that should have been impossible for an Omega. Her body language wasn't submission. It was *war*.

Darius had seen enough battles to recognize a warrior when he saw one. This woman might not have the strength of a high-ranking wolf, but she had something harder. She had the kind of resolve that came from surviving things that should have broken you.

She would need it. Because what was coming—the Lycan Court, the politics, the elders who would see a half-Omega heir as a threat—would test her in ways this human town never had.

Kael was speaking to her, his voice low and rough. Darius couldn't hear all of it, but he caught enough. The King wasn't commanding. He wasn't threatening. He was... *asking*. Lycan King Kael Thorne, who had never asked anyone for anything in his reign, was asking an Omega for permission to know his son.

And she was saying no.

"Five years," Elara said, her voice carrying clearly in the still evening air. "Five years I raised him alone. Five years I fed him, clothed him, protected him, loved him. You don't get to walk into an alley and claim what you threw away."

"Elara—"

"If you take my son, I will burn your Court to the ground." She said it without raising her voice, without a tremor, without a single flicker of doubt. "I don't care if you're the King. I don't care if you have a thousand warriors. I will find a way. Do you understand me?"

Darius believed her.

More importantly, so did Kael.

The King took a step back. Raised both hands, palms out—a gesture of surrender that Darius had never seen him make.

"I'm not taking him from you."

"Then what do you want?"

"I want—" Kael stopped. Swallowed. His jaw worked. "I want to fix what I broke."

Elara stared at him for a long, terrible moment.

"You can't," she said. "Some things don't go back together."

Then she turned, shifted Nox higher on her hip, and walked back into the flower shop without looking back.

Kael stood in the alley, hands still raised, watching her go.

Darius approached carefully. "My King."

"We're not leaving." Kael's voice was hoarse. "Not without them."

"Sir, she—"

"I know what she said." Kael lowered his hands. They were no longer shaking. They were clenched into fists at his sides, and the power radiating off him was enough to make Darius's wolf want to drop to its belly. "But she's not safe here. A royal-grade Lycan child in human territory? Every rogue, every ambitious wolf, every enemy I've made in ten years will eventually sense him. She doesn't have the power to protect him alone."

"She protected him for five years."

"She got lucky for five years." Kael turned, and his eyes were gold fire—not anger, not command, but something rawer. "And I will not let my son's safety depend on luck."

## Chapter 12 — Taken

**ELARA**

They took us three days later.

I'd known they would. Kael Thorne hadn't driven all the way to Ashwood just to stand in an alley and look sad. He was a King. Kings didn't ask. They took.

I just hadn't expected them to come in the middle of the night, while Nox was sleeping, while the shop was dark, while I was finally letting myself believe—stupidly—that maybe they'd left.

I woke to the sound of the back door opening.

Not breaking. *Opening*. With a key. Which meant they'd gotten a copy from the landlord—who was a human and wouldn't have known what he was dealing with, which meant they'd compelled him, which meant these weren't low-level soldiers.

I was out of bed and in Nox's room before the first footstep crossed the threshold. He was sitting up in bed, those gold eyes wide and alert.

"Mama, the wolves are here."

"I know, baby." I grabbed his emergency bag—packed since the day Kael arrived, because I wasn't born yesterday—and lifted him into my arms. "Hold on tight."

I made it as far as the stairs before they blocked me.

Three warriors. Black clothes, neutral expressions, the kind of stillness that spoke of extensive combat training. They didn't draw weapons. They didn't need to.

"Elara of no pack." The one in the center spoke. "By order of Lycan King Kael Thorne, you and the child are to be brought to the Lycan Court."

"I decline."

"This is not a request."

"I said I *decline*." I bared my teeth—something I'd never done in my life, not like this, not with the full force of my wolf behind it. The small, quiet wolf I'd always carried had grown teeth in the past five years. Motherhood did that.

The warrior's expression flickered. Just barely. He hadn't expected resistance from an Omega.

"My orders are to bring you safely," he said. "I would prefer not to use force with the child present."

The mention of Nox—my Nox, my baby, my *everything*—was a scalpel to my heart. They knew exactly where to cut.

I looked at my son. He was watching the warriors with those gold eyes—not afraid, not crying, just *assessing*. Like he was cataloging threats. Like his father did.

"If I go willingly," I said, "you swear no one touches him."

"You have the King's word."

The King's word. The same King who'd sworn the mate bond meant nothing and then spent five years dreaming about me.

"Fine." I pulled Nox tighter. "But if a single one of your warriors looks at my son wrong, I will make you regret it."

The warrior inclined his head. Whether it was respect or just the quickest way to get me moving, I couldn't tell.

They walked us to the SUV. Nox pressed his face into my neck, one small hand clutching the strap of his bag. His little body was tense, coiled—not with fear. With readiness.

His wolf was already awake. Already protecting.

I'd failed to keep him hidden. Failed to keep him safe from the world I'd spent five years escaping.

But I hadn't failed to raise him strong.

That would have to be enough.

## Chapter 13 — Face to Face

The Lycan Court was nothing like I'd imagined.

I'd spent eleven years in the Redfang Pack, which was a functional but uninspiring collection of log buildings around a central meeting hall. The Lycan Court was a *fortress*—stone walls forty feet high, iron gates flanked by carved wolves, courtyards within courtyards, each more imposing than the last.

They brought us through a side entrance—not the main gates, which I suspected was a mercy. Even at midnight, the Court was alive with activity: warriors patrolling, servants carrying linens, the distant howl of a wolf somewhere in the grounds.

My wolf pressed against my consciousness, overwhelmed by the sheer density of power in this place. Every stone was saturated with it. Generations of Lycan Kings had poured their strength into these walls, creating an atmosphere so heavy with authority that my Omega instincts wanted to drop me to my knees.

I didn't kneel. Not anymore.

They brought us to a set of rooms on the third floor of the eastern wing—a sitting room with a fireplace already lit, a bedroom with fresh linens, and a smaller room with a bed sized for a child. Someone had prepared this. Someone had thought about what a four-year-old would need.

The warrior who'd been left at the door—a younger man with a scar across his chin—said, "The King will see you in the morning. Tonight, you should rest."

"Am I a prisoner?"

He hesitated. "You are a guest."

"Guests are allowed to leave."

Another hesitation. "The King would prefer you stay."

"The King can prefer whatever he wants." I set Nox down on the big bed. He was already half-asleep, the journey having drained his small reserves. "I'm not staying because he prefers it. I'm staying because my son needs to be warm tonight."

The warrior left.

I sat on the edge of the bed, watching Nox's chest rise and fall. In sleep, he looked like any other child—soft, small, vulnerable. But even unconscious, his little fingers were curled into fists. His wolf was always on guard.

*My baby. My fierce, impossible baby.*

The door opened.

I was on my feet before it finished moving, positioning myself between the door and the bed.

Kael stood in the doorway.

He'd changed out of the clothes from Ashwood. Now he wore a dark shirt with the sleeves rolled to his elbows, black trousers, his hair slightly damp like he'd recently washed. He looked less like a King and more like a man who hadn't slept in days.

His eyes went past me to the bed. To Nox.

The look on his face—

I wasn't prepared for it. I'd steeled myself against anger, against coldness, against the haughty dismissal of a King who viewed his bastard Omega son as a political inconvenience.

I was not prepared for the naked, raw, *devastated* longing that crossed Kael's features when he saw his child sleeping in that bed.

His jaw clenched. His hands curled at his sides. A muscle in his throat worked, and for a moment—just a moment—he looked less like the most powerful wolf in the world and more like a man standing at the edge of a cliff.

Then he pulled it all back. Locked it down. When he looked at me again, his expression was controlled, careful, empty.

"Is he..." Kael's voice was rough. "Is he healthy?"

"He's perfect."

Something flickered in those gold eyes. Pain, maybe. Or jealousy that I'd gotten to watch him be perfect for five years while Kael hadn't known he existed.

"Elara." He said my name like a confession. "I didn't come here to fight you."

"Then why did you send warriors to take us in the middle of the night?"

"Because you wouldn't have come willingly."

"So you forced me."

"I—" He stopped. Ran a hand over his jaw. "Yes. I forced you. Because you're not safe in that town. Because my enemies would eventually find him. Because every day he's unregistered is a day someone with less honorable intentions could use him."

"So this is about protecting *him*. Not about wanting *me*."

The question hung in the air between us, sharp and ugly and far more vulnerable than I'd meant it to be.

Kael looked at me—really looked, the way he had in the forest five years ago, the way he hadn't been able to look at me under the Blood Moon.

"Wanting you was never the problem," he said quietly.

Then he left, pulling the door shut behind him, and I was left alone with the echo of his words and the phantom warmth of a bond that had no right to still be alive.

## Chapter 14 — The Terms

Morning came too fast.

Nox woke at dawn—his internal clock unbothered by the drama of midnight abductions—and immediately began exploring every inch of the room with the focused intensity of a four-year-old anthropologist. He opened drawers, peeked behind curtains, and pronounced the bathroom "weird because the water comes out of the wall."

A servant brought breakfast: eggs, toast, fruit, and a small plate of cut-up sausages that Nox devoured with a speed that reminded me, uneasily, of his father's appetite.

A knock came at nine.

Kael entered, flanked by Darius, who carried a leather folder thick with documents. The King's face was composed—King mask firmly in place—but his eyes went to Nox immediately.

Nox, sitting cross-legged on the floor with a sausage in each hand, looked up at Kael with an expression of deep suspicion.

"You're the sad wolf," Nox announced.

Darius made a sound that might have been a cough.

Kael crouched down. Not to his knee—too submissive for a King—but enough to bring his face level with Nox's.

"I am. What's your name?"

"Nox." My son studied him with those unnervingly perceptive gold eyes. "My mama says you're the King."

"I am."

"Kings have crowns. Where's your crown?"

"I don't wear it every day."

"Why not?"

"It's heavy."

Nox considered this. "My backpack is heavy too. But I still wear it."

Kael's mouth twitched. The closest thing to a smile I'd seen on his face in five years. "That's a fair point."

"Okay." Nox returned to his sausages, apparently having decided that the King was acceptable company.

I stood frozen against the wall, watching my son accept his father with the casual ease of a child who hadn't yet learned to hold grudges. It was the most painful thing I'd ever witnessed.

Kael rose and turned to me. The warmth was gone from his face, replaced by the careful blankness of negotiation.

"We need to discuss terms."

"Terms," I repeated flatly.

Darius opened the leather folder and withdrew a document. Long, formal, covered in the elaborate script of Lycan legal tradition.

"This is a Mate Contract," Darius explained. "It formalizes the bond between the Lycan King and his chosen mate. In the eyes of the Court, it establishes legitimacy for any offspring."

"You want me to sign a Mate Contract." The words tasted like poison. "After you rejected me."

"I'm not asking you to sign it today," Kael said. "I'm asking you to consider it."

"And if I refuse?"

Darius glanced at Kael. Something passed between them—wordless communication of the kind that only came from years of partnership.

"If you refuse," Kael said, "you're free to leave. But Nox stays."

The floor dropped out from under me.

"He's the Lycan heir," Kael continued, his voice steady and terrible. "By blood, by power, by law. He belongs to the Court. I can't—I won't—let him grow up unprotected in human territory. If you won't stay, he must."

"You're using my son as leverage."

"I'm protecting my bloodline." No hesitation. No apology. The King was back, and he was a wall of iron. "The same way I protect everything that matters."

"He's not a *bloodline*. He's a *child*. He's *my* child."

"He's *our* child." Kael took a step closer. Just one, but it was enough to fill the room with his presence, his power, the gravitational pull of a wolf who'd spent ten years learning to dominate every space he entered. "And I will not apologize for wanting to protect him. Even if that means giving you a choice you don't like."

The choice. Stay and bind myself to the man who destroyed me. Or leave without my son.

He knew I couldn't leave Nox. He knew it the way he knew everything—the way a predator knows exactly where to strike.

"You're a bastard," I said.

"I'm aware."

I looked at my son, happily eating sausages on the floor of his father's fortress, surrounded by power and danger and a man who'd broken my heart without a second thought.

Nox looked up, caught my eye, and smiled. A big, gap-toothed, gold-eyed smile that hit me like sunlight.

*I'll burn the world for you,* I thought. *I'll walk into hell.*

"Fine," I said. "I'll sign your contract. But hear me clearly, Kael Thorne." I met his golden stare with every ounce of steel I had. "I am not your mate. I am not your Luna. I am not your Queen. I am here for my son. The day he's safe enough to leave, I'm gone. And nothing you do—no bond, no contract, no power—will stop me."

Kael held my gaze. Something moved behind his eyes—not victory, not satisfaction. Something more complicated. Something that looked, impossibly, like hope.

"I accept your terms," he said.

The lie between us was thick enough to choke on.

## Chapter 15 — The Court of Wolves

The Lycan Court was a minefield.

I'd been there three days, and I'd already identified at least seven distinct factions, eleven power players, and three people who wanted me dead. And those were just the ones I'd noticed—my Omega senses, while muted, had always been better at reading rooms than reading forests.

The Court operated on a simple principle: power was everything. Every interaction was a negotiation. Every smile was a weapon. Every compliment was a threat wrapped in silk. It was like high school, except if you failed socially, you might get assassinated.

My position was... tenuous.

Officially, I was the King's "guest"—a word that everyone knew meant "captive Omega he's planning to mate." Unofficially, I was the most controversial figure to set foot in the Court in a decade. The Omega who'd been rejected under the Blood Moon. The nobody who'd somehow borne the King's heir. The woman who should have been invisible but was now impossible to ignore.

The servants were polite but distant. The warriors were respectful but watchful. And the Court ladies—wolves of high-ranking families who'd spent years positioning themselves as potential mates for the King—looked at me like I was a rat who'd somehow crawled onto their dinner table.

The worst of them was Seraphina Voss.

She was beautiful—I'd give her that. Tall, curvy, with dark hair that fell in perfect waves and green eyes that calculated your net worth before you'd finished saying hello. Her family was one of the oldest and most powerful in the territory. She'd been the King's intended mate before I'd inconveniently shown up with his son.

She cornered me in the east garden on my third morning, while Nox was with a tutor (a sweet old wolf named Helena who'd been teaching Lycan pups for forty years and didn't bat an eye at a four-year-old with a gold-eyed stare).

"You must be Elara." Seraphina's smile was a scalpel. "The Omega."

"Seraphina." I kept my voice neutral. "The backup plan."

Her eyes flickered. Just barely. Then she laughed—a bright, crystalline sound that probably enchanted every male within earshot.

"I can see why Kael finds you amusing." She stepped closer. She was taller than me by three inches, and she used every one of them. "But let me be clear about something. This—" she gestured at the garden, the Court, the entire world "—is *my* world. I was born for this. You were born to carry water and wash dishes."

"Water carrying builds excellent upper body strength," I said. "You'd be surprised."

Her smile thinned. "The Mate Contract is a formality. A political tool to legitimize the child. Once Nox's position is secured, Kael will need a *real* Luna. Someone with the bloodline, the connections, the *presence* to stand beside a King."

"Someone like you."

"Someone *exactly* like me." She leaned in, and her voice dropped to a whisper. "Enjoy your time in the sun, little Omega. It won't last."

She walked away, her dark hair swaying, her heels clicking on the stone path.

I stood in the garden, surrounded by flowers that weren't mine, in a world that wasn't mine, and I felt the walls closing in.

Seraphina was right about one thing: I wasn't born for this. I was born to be invisible. An Omega. A nothing.

But I'd spent five years building a life from nothing. I'd raised a Lycan heir on my own in a human town with nothing but my wits and my will. I'd stared down the Lycan King in a dark alley and made him step back.

Seraphina Voss thought I was a rat in her dining room.

She was about to learn that rats carried plague.

## Chapter 16 — Unbreakable Bond

**ELARA**

It happened on the seventh night.

Nox had been fine all day—running through the corridors with the kind of reckless energy that only a four-year-old Lycan could generate, playing hide and seek with Darius (who pretended to be terrible at it but was actually letting Nox win), charming the kitchen staff into giving him extra desserts.

Then, at midnight, he started burning up.

I woke to the sound of his cries—not the normal complaints of a child who'd had a nightmare. This was a raw, animal sound, the kind that bypassed the brain and went straight to the spine.

I was at his side in three seconds.

His small body was arched off the bed, his skin burning hot to the touch, his gold eyes squeezed shut. A sheen of sweat covered his forehead, and his silver hair was plastered to his temples.

"Nox! Nox, baby, I'm here—"

His eyes flew open. They weren't gold anymore. They were *white*—blazing, luminous white, the way a wolf's eyes looked during the first shift.

*No. No, he's too young. He's only four—*

"Mama." His voice was small and terrified. "Something's happening. Something's inside me and it won't stop—"

I pulled him into my arms and felt the power rolling through him in waves. It was like holding a live wire—electric, wild, barely contained. His Lycan heritage was awakening, years ahead of schedule, and his small body wasn't ready for it.

"HELP!" I screamed toward the door. "I need help! NOW!"

The door burst open. Not a servant. Not a guard.

Kael.

He must have been awake—must have felt something through the bond that connected him to Nox, the father-child bond that existed independent of the rejected mate bond. He crossed the room in two strides and dropped to his knees beside the bed.

"What's happening?" His voice was sharp, commanding, but I could see the panic beneath it. The same panic I felt.

"His power's awakening. Too early. His body can't handle it."

Kael pressed his hand to Nox's forehead. The moment he made contact, the power in the room *shifted*—like two magnets snapping together. Kael's energy, vast and controlled, wrapped around Nox's wild, uncontained power and held it. Not suppressing it. Containing it. Giving it boundaries.

Nox's body relaxed. His eyes faded from white back to gold. His breathing steadied.

"Daddy," he whispered, and the word hit both of us like a freight train.

It was the first time he'd said it.

Kael's hand trembled against Nox's forehead. His jaw was clenched so tight I could see the muscles jumping. His eyes—those impossible gold eyes—were bright with something I'd never seen in them before.

Tears.

The Lycan King was crying.

Not sobbing, not dramatic—just a brightness, a wetness, a crack in the wall he'd spent ten years building. He blinked it away before Nox could see, but I saw. I saw everything.

"Rest now," Kael said, his voice steady despite the tremor in his hand. "I've got you."

Nox's eyes drifted shut. His small hand found Kael's and held on.

I sat back on my heels and watched the most powerful wolf in the world kneeling beside a child's bed, his hand wrapped around his son's fingers, his power humming through the room like a protective blanket.

For a long time, neither of us spoke.

Then Kael said, without looking at me: "He called me Daddy."

"He did."

Another silence. The fire crackled. Nox's breathing was steady now, deep and even, the power crisis averted.

"I'm sorry, Elara."

The words were so quiet I almost missed them.

"What?"

"I'm sorry." He said it to the wall, to the fire, to the darkness—to everything except me. "For the rejection. For every night you spent alone. For every moment you were afraid and I wasn't there. For not knowing. For not *finding out*."

My throat tightened. "An apology doesn't erase five years."

"No." His hand was still wrapped around Nox's. "But it's a start."

I didn't respond. There was nothing to say—nothing that wouldn't crack open the careful walls I'd built around my heart.

But when Nox shifted in his sleep and his small hand pulled Kael's closer, I didn't pull away.

We stayed like that until dawn—two broken people and the child who'd somehow made them a family, sitting in the dark, bound together by blood and fate and the stubborn, unbreakable thread of a bond that had refused to die.

## Chapter 17 — The Plot

**ELARA**

They tried to kill me in the garden.

It was a week after Nox's power crisis—a week of cautious routine, of navigating Court politics, of watching Seraphina smile at me like a cat watching a mouse explore a trap. I'd started taking walks in the west garden after dinner, when most of the Court was occupied with evening entertainments and the paths were empty.

I should have known better than to be alone.

The first sign was the scent. Every wolf in the Court had a distinct scent signature—part biology, part identity, as unique as a fingerprint. The scent I caught on the evening air was unfamiliar. Not a Court resident. An outsider.

The second sign was the silence. No birds. No insects. Nothing. The garden had gone quiet the way forests go quiet when a predator is near.

I turned to head back inside.

The blade caught the light before I saw the person holding it. A short sword—Lycan-forged, silver-edged, the kind designed to kill wolves. The figure behind it was cloaked, face hidden, but the intent was unmistakable.

I dove to the side. The blade missed my throat by an inch.

My wolf surged forward—not strong enough to shift, but strong enough to sharpen my senses, boost my reflexes. I rolled across the stone path and came up behind a hedge, heart pounding.

The attacker came around the hedge in a rush. I grabbed a fallen branch—thick, heavy, green with sap—and swung it like a bat. It connected with the cloaked figure's arm, and the sword went spinning.

The figure recovered fast. Too fast for a human. This was a wolf—a hired killer, sent by someone in the Court.

A hand wrapped around my throat from behind. I hadn't heard the second one approach.

*Two of them.*

My vision darkened. My lungs screamed. I clawed at the hand, but it was iron, and I was an Omega, and this was how it ended—not in a blaze of glory, but in a garden, strangled by wolves who served someone I'd never even met.

A roar split the night.

Not a human sound. Not even a wolf sound. It was something older, something that resonated in the marrow of every bone in my body—the battle cry of a Lycan King whose territory had been violated.

The hand around my throat vanished. I hit the ground gasping.

When my vision cleared, Kael was standing over the crumpled body of the second attacker. The first was pinned beneath his boot, the silver-edged sword shattered into pieces on the ground.

Kael's eyes were not gold. They were *amber*—the color they turned when his wolf was fully at the surface, when the king and the beast were one.

"Who sent you?" His voice was barely human.

The attacker beneath his boot said nothing.

Kael pressed down. Bones cracked.

"I asked you a question."

"The Voss family," the attacker gasped. "Seraphina Voss. She paid us to kill the Omega and make it look like an accident."

The garden went very still.

Kael's face was something I'd never seen before and hoped never to see again. It was the face of a predator who'd just discovered the location of its prey. It was the face of a King who'd just learned that someone had attempted to murder his mate in his own garden.

It was the face of a wolf about to go to war.

He looked at me. Crouched on the ground, throat bruising, gasping for air.

"Are you hurt?"

I shook my head.

He turned back to the attacker. "Take him to the dungeons. Find his partner. I want names, locations, and every piece of correspondence with the Voss family by sunrise."

Guards materialized—seemingly from the walls themselves—and dragged the attackers away.

Kael knelt beside me. His hands hovered over my throat, not quite touching, his jaw tight with controlled fury.

"Let me see."

I tilted my head. His fingers—gentle, so gentle for hands that had just broken a man's ribs—traced the bruises forming on my neck.

"If you had died—" He stopped. Closed his eyes. His hand was trembling again.

"But I didn't."

"Because I got here in time." His eyes opened. Gold again, not amber, but still burning. "What if I hadn't? What if I'd been too slow? What if—"

"Kael." I said his name for the first time since the rejection—not as an accusation, not as a curse. Just his name. "I'm okay."

He stared at me for a long moment. Then he picked me up—one arm under my knees, one around my back—and carried me inside.

I didn't fight him. I was too tired, too shaken, too aware of how close I'd come to never seeing Nox again.

He carried me all the way to my room, set me on the bed, and called for the healer.

Then he sat in the chair beside my bed and didn't leave until morning.

## Chapter 18 — The Truth

**KAEL**

The investigation took three days.

Three days of interrogation, evidence collection, and the systematic dismantling of everything Seraphina Voss had built within the Court. Her network of informants. Her alliances with minor families. Her carefully cultivated image as the "natural choice" for Luna.

And behind it all—Elder Aldric.

The bastard had been playing a long game. Darius uncovered the first thread on day two: financial records showing payments from the Voss family to Aldric's personal accounts, stretching back eight years. In exchange, Aldric had advocated for the Voss alliance, blocked challenges to Seraphina's position, and quietly steered Court policy in directions that benefited the Voss family's commercial interests.

But the deeper thread was worse.

On day three, Darius walked into Kael's study with a face like granite and a document that made Kael's blood run cold.

"The Silverfang massacre," Darius said, laying the document on the desk. "Fifteen years ago. Aldric was on the Council that approved the military action."

Kael stared at the document. "Silverfang was a rogue pack. They were planning to—"

"They weren't planning anything." Darius's voice was tight. "I found the original intelligence reports. They were fabricated. Aldric fabricated them."

Kael went very still.

"Silverfang Pack was destroyed based on false intelligence," Darius continued. "Every man, woman, and child. Forty-seven wolves. Murdered."

A cold certainty settled in Kael's stomach. "Why?"

"Because of the bloodline." Darius pulled another document from the folder. "Silverfang wolves carried the old royal blood. The *original* royal blood—predating the Lycan King lineage by five hundred years. In Lycan law, the oldest bloodline has the strongest claim to the throne."

Kael's hands went flat on the desk. "Elara."

"Her mother was a Silverfang. Her father was a Silverfang. And Elara—" Darius met his eyes. "Elara is the last of their line. She has a claim to the throne that predates yours, Kael. She's not just an Omega. She's a *Queen*."

The room tilted.

*An apology doesn't erase five years.*

He'd rejected her under the Blood Moon. Called her nothing. Forced her to flee into the human world with his child growing inside her. And all that time, she'd been carrying not just his heir but the blood of the oldest royal lineage in wolf history.

"Aldric ordered the massacre to eliminate the bloodline," Kael said slowly. "And now he wants Elara dead because—"

"Because if she claims her birthright, everything he's built collapses. The Voss alliance, the Council's power structure, his own position. You mated with the most dangerous woman in the world, my King. And you didn't even know it."

Kael stood. The chair crashed backward.

"Bring Aldric to the throne room. Now."

## Chapter 19 — The Choice

The throne room was packed.

Every member of the Court who could walk had squeezed into the great hall—warriors, elders, servants, family heads, all of them drawn by the thunderous energy radiating from the King's seat. Word had spread that something was happening. Something big.

Elara stood at the side of the hall, Nox in Helena's care, watching as Kael took his throne. He wore the Crown today—a circlet of black iron and silver wolf heads that hadn't left the vault in five years. His expression was carved from ice.

Elder Aldric stood in the center of the room, flanked by four guards. His aged face was composed, but his eyes were darting—a cornered rat calculating escape routes.

"Kael," Aldric said, his voice steady despite the tremor in his hands. "What is the meaning of this?"

"You've been charged with conspiracy to commit murder, fabrication of intelligence, and treason against the Crown."

A murmur swept through the crowd. Seraphina, standing near the back in a gown of deep green, went pale.

"These charges are baseless—"

"The evidence says otherwise." Kael nodded at Darius, who produced a stack of documents and began reading. Financial records. Intelligence reports. Correspondence between Aldric and the Voss family. Testimony from the assassins who'd attacked Elara in the garden.

Each revelation sent a new shockwave through the crowd. The Silverfang massacre—an event every wolf had been taught was a justified military action—was revealed as a calculated genocide. The Voss family's rise to power—enabled by Aldric's machinations. The assassination attempt on Elara—ordered and funded by Seraphina herself.

When Darius finished, the silence was absolute.

Kael rose from the throne.

"Elder Aldric. For your crimes against the Crown and against the Silverfang Pack, I strip you of your title, your seat on the Council, and your protection. You will be imprisoned pending trial."

Aldric's composure cracked. "You can't do this! I've served this Court for thirty years—"

"You've *corrupted* this Court for thirty years." Kael's voice dropped to a dangerous register. "And you tried to kill my mate."

The word hit the room like a thunderclap. *Mate.* The King had called her his mate. In public. In the throne room. In front of everyone.

"Seraphina Voss." Kael's gaze swept to the back of the hall. "For conspiracy to commit murder, you are hereby banished from the Lycan Court. Your family's assets are frozen pending investigation. If you return to this territory, you will be executed."

Seraphina's face cycled through denial, rage, and finally, a cold, calculating hatred. She looked directly at Elara—*I'll remember this*—and was escorted from the hall.

Kael descended the steps from the throne. The crowd parted before him like water. He walked until he stood directly in front of Elara.

She looked up at him. Those violet eyes—so guarded, so steel-willed. The eyes of a woman who'd survived everything the world had thrown at her and refused to break.

"Elara of Silverfang." His voice rang through the hall. "I owe you more than an apology. I owe you a throne."

Her brow furrowed. "What are you—"

"The Silverfang bloodline is the oldest royal lineage in wolf history. Older than mine. By rights, by blood, by law—you are not an Omega." He took her hand. "You are a Queen."

Gasps. Whispers. The entire hall erupting into chaos.

But Kael only had eyes for one person. The woman standing before him, her face cycling through shock and disbelief and something that looked dangerously like hope.

"I rejected you under the Blood Moon," he said, loud enough for every wolf in the room to hear. "I called you nothing. I was wrong. You were never nothing, Elara. You were everything. And I was too blind, too proud, too *afraid* to see it."

He lowered himself to one knee.

The Lycan King. On his knee. Before an Omega.

Before his Queen.

"I can't undo the past. I can't give you back the five years you spent alone. I can't erase the pain I caused." He looked up at her, and his eyes—those fierce, golden, terrifying eyes—were soft. Open. Vulnerable. "But I can spend the rest of my life trying to make it right. If you'll let me."

Elara stared down at him. The King on his knee. The most powerful wolf in the world, asking—*asking*—for forgiveness.

"Get up," she said.

Kael's jaw tightened. He began to rise—

"Not yet." She put her hand on his shoulder, keeping him in place. "You don't get to make a grand gesture and walk away clean. You broke me, Kael. You broke me in front of five hundred people, and I spent five years putting myself back together. You don't get to kneel and make that disappear."

"I'm not trying to make it disappear. I'm trying to—"

"Shut up." She wasn't angry. She wasn't cold. Her voice was the voice of a woman who'd walked through fire and come out the other side still standing. "I'll stay. Not because of a contract. Not because of a throne. Not even because of Nox. I'll stay because, despite everything, my wolf still reaches for yours. And I'm tired of pretending she doesn't."

She pulled him to his feet.

"But you're going to earn this, Kael Thorne. Every day. For the rest of your life."

He looked at her. She looked at him. The bond—the shattered, ghostly, stubbornly persistent bond between them—stirred. Not fully awake. Not yet. But warmer. Closer. Alive.

"Every day," he agreed.

## Chapter 20 — Burn the World

**KAEL**

He'd made his choice.

Now he had to live with it.

The weeks after the throne room were a blur of political maneuvering. Aldric's trial was swift and merciless—the evidence was overwhelming, and the Elder spent his last days in a cell beneath the Court, stripped of everything he'd spent thirty years building.

The Voss family's assets were seized. Their allies scattered like roaches when the light came on. Seraphina was gone—banished to the outer territories, her golden future reduced to ash and exile.

And Elara...

Elara stayed.

Not in his chambers. Not yet. She'd taken a suite in the eastern wing, close to Nox's room but far from Kael's, and she'd made it clear that proximity did not equal permission.

But she stayed. She ate meals with him. She walked the gardens with Nox between them. She argued with him about Court policy and training protocols for Nox's developing powers and whether the cook's stew needed more rosemary.

She *stayed*.

The mate bond was healing. Slowly. Not the dramatic, cinematic reunion he might have imagined—more like a wound closing stitch by stitch, each day a little less painful, a little more whole. He could feel her emotions now, faintly: her warmth when Nox laughed, her tension in crowded rooms, her grudging awareness of *him* that she tried very hard to ignore.

She was ignoring him less.

One evening, three weeks after the throne room, she appeared at his study door.

"Your training master is terrible," she announced.

"My training master has been training Lycan heirs for twenty years."

"Then he's been doing it wrong for twenty years. Nox needs structure, not brutality. He's four, not forty."

"He broke a table yesterday. With his bare hands. While laughing."

"He's *energetic*."

"He's a *hurricane*."

Elara's mouth twitched. The closest thing to a smile he'd seen from her that wasn't for Nox.

They stood in the doorway, arguing about their son, and it was so achingly *normal* that Kael wanted to freeze the moment and live in it forever.

"Come to dinner," he said. "Not as a political arrangement. Not as a strategy. Just... dinner. With me."

She studied him with those violet eyes—assessing, weighing, measuring the distance between them.

"One dinner," she said.

"One dinner."

"If you try anything—"

"I won't."

"—I will stab you with a salad fork."

"Noted."

She came to dinner. And the next night. And the night after that.

They talked. About Nox, about the Court, about her flower shop and whether roses could grow in the Court's climate (they could; he ordered a greenhouse built the next morning). About her childhood before the massacre—the few fragments she remembered. About his father, the King he'd barely known before the assassination.

They didn't talk about the bond. Not yet. It was the elephant in every room—a warmth that grew stronger each day, a pull that both of them felt and neither of them named.

But it was there. Growing. Healing.

And on the night of the full moon—exactly one month after the throne room—Elara came to his door.

Not to argue. Not to discuss Nox.

"Tell me this isn't just the bond," she said, standing in the corridor with her silver hair loose and her violet eyes uncertain. "Tell me this is real."

He stepped forward. Close enough to touch. Close enough to feel the heat radiating off her skin, the hitch in her breath, the way her wolf pressed toward his through the bond.

"I rejected the bond," he said. "I severed it. Destroyed it. And I still spent five years unable to forget the taste of your name. That's not the bond, Elara. That's *me*."

She searched his face—looking for the lie, the strategy, the King's calculation.

She didn't find it.

She kissed him.

Not like the forest—desperate, reckless, a collision of instinct and accident. This was deliberate. A choice. Her hands on his jaw, her lips soft and sure, her body melting into his like she was coming home after five years in the cold.

The bond *ignited*.

Not the ghost. Not the whisper. The real thing—the full, blazing, undeniable mate bond, roaring back to life between them like a bonfire that had been waiting for a single spark. He felt her—every inch of her, every emotion, every wall she'd built and every crack in them.

She felt him, too. The grief. The guilt. The desperate, aching love he'd been carrying for five years like a stone in his chest.

*I love you,* the bond whispered. *I've loved you since the forest. I loved you when I rejected you. I loved you when I thought I'd never see you again.*

*I know,* she whispered back, through the bond, through the kiss, through everything. *I know.*

## Epilogue — Blood Moon Rising

**ELARA**

One month later.

Nox ran through the courtyard, chasing a butterfly with the single-minded determination of a four-year-old who had recently discovered that the world was full of wonders and he intended to catch every single one.

I sat on the stone bench, watching him, my hand resting on the arm that had—sometime in the past few weeks—learned to seek out Kael's warmth without conscious thought.

Kael sat beside me. His hand covered mine. His thumb traced absent patterns on my skin.

The Court had accepted me. Not because of the Silverfang bloodline (though that helped). Not because of Kael's declaration (though that silenced the loudest doubters). They accepted me because of Nox—because a boy with gold eyes and silver hair ran through their corridors and called their King "Daddy" and made the fiercest warriors in the territory play hide and seek.

They accepted me because I'd earned it.

The bond was whole now. Not perfect—there were still cracks, still echoes of the rejection that flared on quiet nights, still moments when I pulled away and he let me. But whole. Alive. Growing stronger every day.

I was his Luna. His Queen. His mate.

And I was still Elara—still the Omega who'd been rejected under the Blood Moon, still the woman who'd raised her son alone, still the wolf who'd stared down a King and made him kneel.

That would never change.

"Mama!" Nox ran up to us, butterfly-free and grinning. "Can I have ice cream?"

"You just had ice cream."

"But I want *more* ice cream."

"Absolutely not."

He turned to Kael with those gold eyes—the same strategic pivot he used every time I said no.

"Daddy—"

"Don't look at me. Your mother is the Luna. Luna's word is law."

Nox's face scrunched in frustration. "That's not fair."

"Life's not fair, kid." Kael lifted him onto his lap. "But I'll tell you a secret."

Nox leaned in, conspiratorial.

"Your mother can't resist the puppy eyes. Give it three minutes and ask again."

I swatted Kael's arm. "You're undermining my authority."

"You don't need authority. You have the puppy eyes defense."

"I hate you."

"No, you don't."

He kissed me—brief, warm, casual—the kind of kiss that came from living with someone, from building a life together, from choosing each other every day.

Nox made a gagging sound. "Gross."

"You'll understand when you're older," Kael said.

"I'll *never* kiss anyone. Kissing is *disgusting*."

"Sure, kid. Sure."

I leaned into Kael's shoulder, my son in his lap, my hand in his, and for one perfect moment, everything was exactly as it should be.

But that night, I dreamed.

I stood in a dark forest—not the forest of my childhood, not the woods behind Redfang, but somewhere older, deeper, wilder. The trees were black against a red sky, and the moon was blood-red and pulsing.

My mother stood before me.

Not as I remembered her—not the terrified woman who'd hidden me in a hollow tree. This was a different version of her: tall, strong, her silver hair flowing like water, her violet eyes blazing with power.

"Elara." Her voice was the wind and the earth and the howl of every wolf that had ever lived. "The war hasn't begun yet, my daughter."

"What war?"

"The war for everything. For blood. For truth. For the throne that was always yours." She reached out and touched my cheek. Her hand was warm. "Your real test is coming. And you won't survive it by hiding."

"I'm not hiding anymore."

"No," she agreed, and smiled. "You're not. But there are those who will try to make you. And there are secrets in your blood that even the Moon Goddess hasn't revealed yet."

"What secrets?"

She began to fade. The forest dissolved. The blood moon dimmed.

"Wake up, Elara," she whispered. "Wake up and remember who you are."

I woke with a gasp.

The room was dark. Kael slept beside me, his breathing steady, his arm wrapped around my waist. Nox was safe in the next room, his small body warm under his blankets.

Everything was fine.

Everything was fine.

But my mother's pendant—the silver crescent I'd worn for eighteen years—was warm against my chest. Warmer than it had ever been.

And in the darkness, I heard something I hadn't heard since the night my pack was destroyed.

A howl.

Not a wolf. Not a man. Something older.

Something calling me home.
